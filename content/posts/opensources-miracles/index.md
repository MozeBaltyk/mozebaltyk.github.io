This article is a tribute to some crazy and awesome open-source projects that compete with, and sometimes outperform, commercial products. Their success shows what passionate individuals, communities, universities, and companies can achieve when they build in the open.

Here are a few of them...

## Linux

The first one is Linux, which powers much of the cloud and is becoming more popular with the general public. Proprietary Unix systems such as AIX and Solaris once dominated enterprise computing but have since lost much of their influence. Seeing Microsoft support the **Linux Foundation** shows just how much the technology world has changed.

Its success comes from collaboration: individuals, universities, and competing companies all improve the same kernel. Linux now runs everything from tiny devices and smartphones to supercomputers, proving that an open project can become essential infrastructure.

Another strength of Linux is its flexibility. It follows the Unix philosophy of building tools that do one thing well and can be combined to solve larger problems. The kernel supports loadable modules, while distributions let users choose and replace many parts of the system according to their needs.

Together, Linux and its surrounding ecosystem showed the world how open collaboration and small, focused components can create powerful and flexible systems.

## Git

Git was created by Linus Torvalds (the same guy who created Linux, by the way) to support Linux kernel development. It is fast, distributed, and resilient: every developer has a complete copy of the repository and its history. Today, Git is the standard tool for version control and the foundation of platforms such as GitHub and GitLab.

Git also changed how we think about collaboration. It taught us to work through small, traceable, and reversible changes instead of exchanging final versions of files. Mistakes became less frightening because previous versions could be recovered, while branches made it possible to experiment without disrupting other people's work.

Its distributed model gave every contributor a complete copy of a project and the freedom to work independently. The ecosystem built around Git lowered the barrier to contribution through forks, code reviews, and pull requests. Git showed that collaboration does not require constant coordination or a single central authority; it requires a shared history and a reliable way to combine ideas.

## A Foundation for IT Education

Let's pause here for a moment. These two open-source projects alone represent more than technical achievements. In my view, Linux and Git should be part of every school's IT curriculum because they teach the mindset and foundational skills that future engineers need.

Linux provides a foundation for understanding operating systems and containerisation. Its ecosystem is ideal for learning programming, while its many distributions introduce students to systems designed for different purposes. It is also a gateway to open-source educational software.

Git, meanwhile, teaches collaboration. Together, Linux and Git can become two pillars of a successful and fulfilling career—and perhaps of a life that brings satisfaction through the feeling that we are contributing something useful to society.

These lessons are useful far beyond software development. Whether students later specialise in infrastructure, electronics, science, design, or another engineering field, Linux and Git give them a strong foundation on which to build.

## OBS Studio

OBS Studio is a free tool for recording videos and broadcasting live streams. Despite competing with expensive commercial software, it has become a favourite among streamers, teachers, and content creators because it is powerful, cross-platform, and supported by a large community.

Plugins and integrations allow users to adapt it to many workflows, from a simple screen recording to a professional production with several cameras and audio sources.

More importantly, OBS Studio made broadcasting accessible. Work that once required a television studio and specialised equipment can now be done by an individual with a computer, giving more people the ability to teach, entertain, and share their ideas.

## Blender

Blender is one of the most impressive open-source success stories. It provides professional tools for 3D modelling, animation, visual effects, video editing, and rendering, all in a single application that anyone can download and use.

Its development is supported by artists, studios, and technology companies. Blender shows that open-source software can compete at the highest level while making creative tools available to people who could not afford traditional commercial alternatives.

## Conclusion

These projects solve very different problems, but their success follows the same pattern: people share their work, others improve it, and the whole community benefits. Their real miracle is not that the software is free of charge, but that knowledge, tools, and the power to create are open to everyone.
